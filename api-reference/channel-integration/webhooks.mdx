title: "Webhooks"
description: "Get notified when availability, contacts, memberships or charges change."
---

Webhooks push changes to your app in near real time. 1club sends a signed HTTP
`POST` to your endpoint when something you subscribed to changes, whichever channel
made the change: your app, the gym's own tools, another partner, a job or an import.

There are two kinds of webhook events:

- **Availability** (`availability.changed`): a court's bookable availability changed. Use it to show a live calendar instead of discovering conflicts only when a booking is rejected.
- **Contacts, memberships and charges** (`contact.updated`, `membership.paused`, `charge.paid`, ...): a record was created, changed or deleted. Use them to keep a CRM, a data warehouse or a billing system in step with the gym. See [Contact, membership and charge events](#contact-membership-and-charge-events).

For availability, webhooks are the low-latency signal and the [change feed](/api-reference/channel-integration/areas-and-availability#availability-change-feed)
is the correctness backstop. Build for both (see [Staying in sync](/api-reference/channel-integration/overview#staying-in-sync)).

## Quickstart: a live calendar

Build a live calendar in five steps:

1. **Baseline.** Pull the club grid once to render the calendar and store the returned `cursor`:
   `GET /v1/platform/availability?clubId=8&from=…&to=…`.
2. **Register** an HTTPS endpoint (below) and store the `whsec_…` signing secret.
3. **Receive & verify.** On each `POST`, check the `1Club-Signature` header against the raw body ([verify the signature](#verify-the-signature)) and respond `2xx` within 10 seconds.
4. **Re-pull.** Fetch the affected window named in the event's `data` and update those slots:
   `GET /v1/platform/areas/{areaId}/availability?from=…&to=…`. Keep the highest `version`/`sequence` you've applied.
5. **Reconcile.** On a slow timer - and after any downtime - call `GET /v1/platform/availability/changes?since=<cursor>`, apply the deltas, and store the new `cursor`.

Steps 3-4 keep the calendar fresh in seconds; steps 1 and 5 keep it correct even if a webhook is never delivered.

## Register an endpoint

In the admin dashboard under **Settings** > **Integrations** > **APIs & webhooks**, add a webhook endpoint:
an HTTPS URL to receive events, and the event types it receives. On creation you get a **signing secret**
(`whsec_...`) shown **once** - store it securely; you'll use it to verify every
delivery. You can rotate the secret, change its event types or disable the endpoint
at any time. An organization can have up to 10 endpoints, and managing them requires
the **settings.manage.extended** permission.

A new endpoint starts with `availability.changed` only. Tick each contact, membership
and charge type you want: subscribe only to the types your integration handles, because
a type your endpoint keeps rejecting counts as failed deliveries and can get the
endpoint disabled (see [Reliability](#reliability)).

<Note>
  Webhooks are part of the paid API feature. Endpoints must use `https`.
</Note>

## The `availability.changed` event

Its payload is a **thin invalidation**: it names the area and
window that changed and why - not the new availability itself. On receipt, re-pull
[`GET /availability`](/api-reference/channel-integration/areas-and-availability) for
that area/window to get the authoritative state. This keeps 1club the source of truth
and makes events safe to process out of order.

```json
{
  "id": "evt_9f2c4a1b8d7e4c2fa0b1c2d3e4f5a6b7",
  "type": "availability.changed",
  "occurredAt": "2026-07-20T16:32:04.115Z",
  "sequence": "48213",
  "organizationId": 8,
  "data": {
    "clubId": 8,
    "areaId": 51,
    "window": { "from": "2026-07-20T17:00:00.000Z", "to": "2026-07-20T18:00:00.000Z" },
    "reason": "booking.created"
  }
}
```

- **`id`** - unique per event. Deduplicate on it (delivery is at-least-once).
- **`sequence`** - the change's position in your org's change feed. Compare it to the `version` on an availability read to know whether your last read already reflects this change; use it as the `since` cursor for the [change feed](/api-reference/channel-integration/areas-and-availability#availability-change-feed).
- **`data.clubId`** - the gym the area belongs to. It can be `null`.
- **`data.reason`** - a coarse cause label. Values today:
  - Bookings: `booking.created`, `booking.updated`, `booking.cancelled`, `booking.released`, `booking.reinstated`, `booking.relocated`
  - Classes: `class.reserved`, `class.reservation_removed`
  - Events: `event.published`, `event.unpublished`, `event.updated`, `event.cancelled`, `event.deleted`
  - Areas and area types: `area.created`, `area.updated`, `area.deleted`, `area_type.updated`
  - Area blocks: `area_block_created`, `area_block_updated`, `area_block_deleted`
  - Seasons: `season_slot.moved`, `season_pass.cancelled`

  The list grows as new kinds of change are tracked. Treat it as a hint, never branch on an exhaustive list, and always re-pull for truth.

<Note>
  `data.window` is the affected time range, but for configuration changes (such
  as `area.updated`, when operating hours or capacity change, or `area_type.updated`)
  it is a **coarse, forward-looking window** of about 62 days from the change - not
  a single slot. Always re-pull
  `/availability` for the window rather than assuming exactly one slot changed.
</Note>

### `sequence`, `version`, and `cursor`

Three related tokens tie reads, webhooks, and the change feed together. All are
monotonically increasing integers **sent as strings** (they can exceed `2^53`, so
don't parse them into a JS `number` - compare as strings of equal length, or as
`BigInt`).

| Token | Scope | Where you see it | Use it to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sequence` | one change | webhook `sequence`, change-feed `sequence` | order changes / detect gaps; pass as the `since` cursor |
| `version` | one area | availability read (`version`, and the `ETag`) | tell whether a read already reflects a given `sequence` |
| `cursor` | whole org | grid `cursor`, change-feed `cursor` | your reconciliation checkpoint |

## Contact, membership and charge events

These events tell you that a record changed, not what it now holds. The payload names
the record and carries its ids and statuses, never a person's details. On receipt,
read the record itself with your API key:

- a contact: [`GET /v1/platform/contacts/{id}`](/api-reference/channel-integration/contacts#open-a-contact) (`contacts:read`)
- a membership: [`GET /v1/platform/memberships/{id}`](/api-reference/channel-integration/memberships#get-a-membership) (`memberships:read`)
- a charge: `GET /v1/platform/transactions/{id}` (`transactions:read`). Charges are called transactions in the API.

They come from every place a record can change: the admin, the member portal and
app, the check-in desk, imports, scheduled renewals and the platform API itself.

### Event types

| Type | Sent when |
| --- | --- |
| `contact.created` | A contact is added. |
| `contact.updated` | The contact's name, email, phone, address, date of birth, gender or contact type changes. |
| `contact.suspended` | The gym suspends the contact. |
| `contact.reinstated` | The suspension is lifted. |
| `contact.deleted` | The contact is deleted, for example when it is merged into another one. |
| `membership.created` | A membership is sold or added. |
| `membership.activated` | The membership becomes active, for example once it is paid. |
| `membership.paused` | The membership is paused. |
| `membership.resumed` | A paused membership is active again. |
| `membership.cancelled` | The membership is cancelled. |
| `membership.expired` | The membership reaches its end date. |
| `membership.used` | A pass uses up its last session. |
| `membership.updated` | Its plan, holder, gym, dates, renewal, price, billing frequency, scheduled cancellation, commitment or pause end changes, or its status moves to one no other type names (such as pending). |
| `membership.deleted` | The membership is deleted. |
| `charge.created` | A charge is raised. |
| `charge.paid` | The charge is paid in full. |
| `charge.failed` | A payment for the charge failed. |
| `charge.refunded` | The charge is refunded in full. |
| `charge.voided` | The charge is voided or cancelled. |
| `charge.updated` | Its amount, tax, date, due date, description, revenue account, or the contact, membership or booking it belongs to changes, or its status moves to one no other type names (such as partially paid or overdue). |
| `charge.deleted` | The charge is deleted. |

A change sends one event. A record created already in a named status sends two: a
sale paid at the till sends `charge.created`, then `charge.paid`. A write that
changes none of the fields above sends nothing.

### Payload

```json
{
  "id": "evt_5e5619c8c24d48ceb8bcc7d4bd9335c0",
  "type": "membership.paused",
  "occurredAt": "2026-11-08T10:44:52.311Z",
  "sequence": "33",
  "organizationId": 8,
  "data": {
    "object": "membership",
    "id": 41,
    "contactId": 15,
    "planId": 4,
    "status": "paused",
    "previousStatus": "active",
    "changedFields": ["status"]
  }
}
```

- **`id`** - unique per event, and the same on every endpoint the event goes to. Deduplicate on it.
- **`sequence`** - orders the events of one record: a later change to the same record always has a higher `sequence`, whatever order retries deliver them in. It is not a change-feed cursor. Like availability's, it is an integer sent as a string.
- **`data.object`** and **`data.id`** - the record: `contact`, `membership` or `charge`, and its id.
- **`data.changedFields`** - on an update, the names of the fields that changed. Never their values.
- **Memberships** also carry `contactId`, `planId`, `status` and, when the status changed, `previousStatus` (`null` when the membership was created in that status).
- **Charges** also carry `contactId`, `membershipId`, `bookingId`, `paymentStatus` and, when the status changed, `previousPaymentStatus` (`null` when the charge was created in that status).

<Note>
  A partial refund leaves the charge partly paid, so it arrives as `charge.updated`
  with the new `paymentStatus`. Only a full refund sends `charge.refunded`.
</Note>

### Recorded only while you subscribe

1club records these events only while one of the organization's endpoints
subscribes to the type. A change made before you ticked a type is never sent. There
is no change feed for these events yet, so after an outage longer than the retry
window, or while an endpoint was disabled, re-read the contacts, memberships and
charges you keep with the list endpoints to catch up.

## Verify the signature

Every delivery includes a timestamped HMAC-SHA256 signature over the raw request
body, so you can confirm it came from 1club and reject replays.

Headers:

| Header | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `1Club-Signature` | `t=<unix-seconds>,v1=<hex>` |
| `1Club-Event-Id` | The event `id` (also in the body) |
| `1Club-Event-Type` | The event `type`, such as `availability.changed` or `charge.paid` |
| `1Club-Delivery-Attempt` | Attempt number, starting at 1 |

The `v1=` element is the signature-scheme version. If a future scheme is added it
will be sent as an extra element (e.g. `v2=`) alongside `v1=`, so parse by name and
verify the scheme you support rather than assuming a fixed layout.

The signed string is `"<t>.<raw-body>"`. Verify with a constant-time compare and
reject timestamps outside a tolerance (e.g. 5 minutes):

```ts
import crypto from "node:crypto";

function verify(rawBody: string, header: string, secret: string, toleranceSec = 300): boolean {
  const parts = Object.fromEntries(header.split(",").map(kv => kv.split("=")));
  const t = Number.parseInt(parts.t, 10);
  if (!Number.isFinite(t) || Math.abs(Date.now() / 1000 - t) > toleranceSec) return false;

  const expected = crypto.createHmac("sha256", secret).update(`${t}.${rawBody}`).digest("hex");
  const a = Buffer.from(parts.v1, "hex");
  const b = Buffer.from(expected, "hex");
  return a.length === b.length && crypto.timingSafeEqual(a, b);
}
```

<Note>
  Verify against the **raw** request body, before any JSON parsing/re-serialization
  - a re-encoded body will not match the signature.
</Note>

## Delivery, retries, and idempotency

### Delivery timing

Changes are dispatched by a worker that runs **every 30 seconds**, so a first
delivery normally arrives within about half a minute of the change, plus network
time. Treat the integration as near-real-time rather than instant: design the
booking flow so a court can be sold on either side during that window, and let the
[atomic re-check on create](/api-reference/channel-integration/bookings#conflicts)
be what stops a double-booking.

<Warning>
  Do not measure delivery latency against a **staging** or trial environment. Those
  run with CPU allocated only while serving requests, which starves the dispatch
  worker between ticks and inflates the figure well past what production does.
</Warning>

### Reliability

- **Respond within 10 seconds.** Any `2xx` marks the delivery successful. A non-`2xx`, a connection error, or a response slower than the **10-second** timeout counts as a failure. Do the minimum synchronously (verify, enqueue, ack) and re-pull availability or the changed record asynchronously.
- **At-least-once.** You may receive the same `id` more than once. Deduplicate on `id` and make handling idempotent (re-reading availability or a record already is).
- **Retries with backoff.** A failed delivery is retried up to **7 attempts total** - the first immediately, then after roughly 1m, 5m, 30m, 2h, 6h, and 24h (about **33 hours** end to end). Each attempt runs on the next tick of the 30-second dispatch worker, so it can arrive up to about half a minute after its scheduled time.
- **Auto-disable.** After the 7th attempt fails, the endpoint is **disabled automatically** and the reason is shown under **Settings** > **Integrations** > **APIs & webhooks**. While an endpoint is disabled, new changes are not queued for it. Re-enable it (and rotate the secret if needed) once healthy, then catch up on everything that changed while it was down: availability from the change feed, contacts, memberships and charges by re-reading them (see [Reconcile after downtime](#reconcile-after-downtime)).
- **Ordering is not guaranteed.** Use `sequence` if you need to order events: for availability it also detects gaps, and for contacts, memberships and charges it orders one record's events. Because every event is an invalidation you re-read for, exact order rarely matters.

## Reconcile after downtime

If your endpoint was down (or auto-disabled), don't try to replay individual
webhooks - pull the [change feed](/api-reference/channel-integration/areas-and-availability#availability-change-feed)
from your last stored `cursor`, apply the changes, and store the new cursor. Polling
the feed on a slow timer (e.g. every few minutes) in addition to webhooks means your
calendar self-heals from any missed delivery.

**First sync (no cursor yet):** do the baseline grid pull ([Quickstart](#quickstart-a-live-calendar)
step 1) and start from the `cursor` it returns - don't start from `0` for a live
club, or you'll replay the entire history.

**Very long outages:** the feed serves a rolling history. If your stored `cursor`
is older than the retained history, the feed can't close the whole gap - discard
the cursor and re-baseline with a full grid pull, exactly like first sync. When in
doubt, a full grid re-pull is always a safe way to resynchronise.

**Contacts, memberships and charges** have no change feed yet. Missed events are not
replayed, so after downtime re-read the records you keep: list the contacts and
memberships you sync, and open the charges you track. A failed delivery is still
retried for about 33 hours, so a short outage loses nothing.

## Troubleshooting

- **Deliveries stopped arriving.** The endpoint was likely auto-disabled after repeated failures - check **Settings** > **Integrations** > **APIs & webhooks** for the disable reason, fix your endpoint, re-enable it, then reconcile from your last `cursor` and re-read the records you keep.
- **A contact, membership or charge event never arrives.** Check that its type is ticked on the endpoint, and that the change touched a field the event covers (see [Event types](#event-types)). Changes made before you ticked the type are never sent.
- **Signature always fails.** You're almost certainly hashing a re-serialized body - verify against the exact bytes received (see the note above), and confirm you're using the current secret (rotating it invalidates the old one immediately).
- **Correlate and dedupe on your side.** Log the `1Club-Event-Id` and `1Club-Delivery-Attempt` headers: the event id lets you dedupe, and the attempt number tells first deliveries apart from retries.
