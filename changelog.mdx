  ## Resend your calendar invitation

  Instructors can put a class or personal training session back in their own calendar without asking an admin to re-save it.

  When a class or a personal training session is scheduled, the instructor gets

<Update label="2026-08-11" description="Bookable packages" tags={["New","Highlight","Billing"]}>
  ## Bookable packages

  Sell a birthday party or a team building day from a signup form, and let the customer pick the date themselves.

  A package can now be sold against a time you actually run it, instead of the
  club phoning the customer back to agree a date.

  Turn on "Bookable" on any package, choose the days of the week it runs, and
  list the times you start it - 10:00 and 14:00, say. Everything else about the
  package stays as it was: the plans and products it includes, the headcount,
  the price per extra child.

  On the signup form the customer sees the free times next to the package,
  picks one while they are choosing what to buy, and confirms the date on the
  review screen before paying. Only times you actually offer appear, and days
  the package does not run are greyed out. A time already sold disappears, so
  two customers cannot book the same Saturday afternoon - the second one is
  told the slot has gone rather than finding out on the day.

  Staff see the date wherever the order is: on the order line in the admin, and
  in the confirmation email the customer keeps. Cancelling the order gives the
  date back, so it goes straight back on sale.

  Built for parties, team building and private hire - anything where one
  customer takes the whole thing rather than buying a ticket alongside others.
</Update>

  ## Cancelling a paid order refunds it

  Cancelling an order that has been paid now returns the money instead of just voiding the sale.

  Cancelling a paid order used to void its transactions. The sale left the
  books, but nothing gave the money back - the club kept it and there was no
  record that it was owed.

  Cancelling now asks where the refund should go and returns the money through
  the same path as a refund from the transaction screen. The dialog shows the
  amount before you commit, and the destination it can actually reach: back to
  the original card, or as account credit. Payments taken through a provider
  that cannot be reversed automatically say so, and offer account credit
  instead of quietly turning a card payment into credit without telling you.

  An order with nothing settled on it still cancels in one step, exactly as
  before. An order that has been paid can no longer be cancelled without
  deciding what happens to the money.

<Update label="2026-08-05" description="Export to Excel" tags={["New","Settings"]}>
  ## Export to Excel

  Every export and report now downloads as a real Excel workbook — dates, amounts and phone numbers arrive as themselves, not as text.

  Exports used to come out as CSV only. That opens in Excel, but not always the
  way you want: in languages where Excel expects a semicolon between values, a
  comma-separated file lands entirely in the first column, and everything in it
  arrives as text — so amounts will not add up, dates will not sort, and a phone
  number can lose its leading "+".

  Every export now offers Excel (.xlsx) alongside CSV. Pick the format in the
  export wizard, or from the Export button on any report or dashboard. Excel is
  the default; CSV is one click away whenever another system needs it.

  In an Excel export the cells carry their real types. Amounts are numbers you
  can sum and format as currency, dates are dates you can sort and filter by,
  and identifiers that only look like numbers — phone numbers, postal codes,
  invoice references — stay exactly as they were entered. The header row is
  bold and stays visible while you scroll, and columns are sized to their
  contents, so the file is ready to read the moment it opens.

  The accounting export follows the same choice. As CSV it stays the ZIP of
  separate files your accountant's import tool expects; as Excel it becomes one
  workbook with a sheet per document type — customers, sales receipts, invoices,
  credit memos — plus the read-me explaining what to import and in what order.

  Very large exports stay on CSV: an Excel workbook is assembled in one piece,
  so above 50,000 rows the export asks you to narrow the date range or take the
  CSV instead.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-08-05" description="Staying signed in through brief service interruptions" tags={["Fixed","Settings"]}>
  ## Staying signed in through brief service interruptions

  Short-lived server or connection problems no longer end your session — you stay signed in and the page keeps working.

  Some staff were being returned to the sign-in screen while they were still
  working, sometimes repeatedly through the day, with the message that their
  session had expired.

  Sessions renew quietly in the background every few minutes. If that renewal
  couldn't complete — a brief database or connectivity problem, or a routine
  deployment — it was treated the same as a session that had genuinely ended, and
  you were signed out. Now a renewal that simply didn't finish is retried, your
  session is kept, and only a session that has really ended sends you back to
  sign-in.

  Two related causes are fixed alongside it: a browser that had an out-of-date
  copy of its session could get stuck in the same loop after every sign-in, and
  staff working across several tabs no longer lose their session when the tabs
  renew at the same moment.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-07-30" description="Scan barcodes at the point of sale" tags={["New","Billing"]}>
  ## Scan barcodes at the point of sale

  Plug a barcode scanner into the till to add items to an order, or scan a member card to see at once whether the member is valid.

  Any keyboard-style USB or Bluetooth scanner gun works at the point of sale.
  There is nothing to install, nothing to pair inside 1club, and no scan mode to
  switch on. The till listens for a scanner the whole time it is open, wherever
  the cashier's hands are.

  Scan a product and it drops into the order, ready to charge. Membership plans,
  passes, and packages can carry a barcode as well, so a stickered day pass rings
  up exactly the way a drink does. A code means one thing inside your
  organization: if you try to save a barcode that is already on another item or on
  a member card, the save is refused and you are told what already holds it.

  Scan a member's card and the till answers the question the desk actually asks:
  may this person be let in right now. You get one of three verdicts, each with its
  reason. Valid means they hold a membership the desk can admit them on. Needs
  attention means they are a current member but nothing they hold is
  straightforwardly usable, for example the sessions are spent or money is owed.
  Not valid means the card resolves to nobody usable. From the same panel you can
  add that member to the order in one click, or open their profile.

  Scanning a card is a lookup, not a check-in. Nothing is written against the
  member, no session is spent, and no attendance is recorded, so admitting someone
  stays a separate and deliberate step.

  [Learn more](/pos/point-of-sale)
</Update>

  Partner platforms can now read a whole club's open slots in one call and receive webhooks the moment a court's availability changes, so their calendars stay live.

  Partners selling your courts through the channel integration can now keep a
  truly live calendar.

  The availability API returns ready-made open slots — with remaining capacity
  and the club's timezone — for a single court or a whole club in one request,
  so partners no longer have to stitch together operating hours and busy
  intervals themselves.

  New availability webhooks push a signed event within seconds whenever a court
  is booked, freed, or blocked on any channel — your own tools, another partner,
  a class, or an event. Partners register an endpoint under Settings → APIs and
  MCP, verify the signature, and re-pull the affected slots. A reconciliation
  feed lets them catch up exactly after any missed delivery, so availability
  never drifts and double-bookings are still impossible.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-07-10" description="Self-service Check-in App" tags={["New","Highlight","Operations"]}>
  ## Self-service Check-in App

  Members can now check themselves into classes or the gym from a dedicated self-service screen at the club.

  A new touch-first Check-in App runs on a tablet or screen at your club, next to
  the existing kiosk. Members find themselves by name (or with their QR code or
  PIN, when enabled) and are checked in instantly - a green screen confirms the
  check-in, an amber screen means they are in but a payment is still due, and a
  red screen sends them to the front desk.

  - Members can hold the QR code from their 1club app up to the tablet's camera
    and are checked in on the spot, without touching the screen.
  - Members with an upcoming booking are checked in the moment they identify
    themselves.
  - Without a booking, members pick from the classes starting in the next hour;
    the club's booking policy decides whether their membership covers it or an
    unpaid payment is recorded for the front desk.
  - Choose the journey per club: identify first, or pick a class first and tap
    your name on the class list.
  - Open Gym mode checks members in on their general membership with no class
    list at all.

  Configure everything under Settings, Member Experience, Check-in App: enable it
  per club, copy the device link, toggle QR and PIN identification and Open Gym
  mode, and drag to reorder the user journey.
</Update>
