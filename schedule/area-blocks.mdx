A block takes an area, or a whole gym, off the bookable schedule for a specific period. Owners and managers create blocks for anything with a start and an end, and members are simply not offered the blocked slots. A block is the area equivalent of an instructor's time off.

## When to use it

Because a block has an end, the area reopens on its own - you don't have to remember to undo it. While it runs, the closed period stops new bookings on the admin schedule, the member portal and app, the marketplace, and the API. Bookings already in the period stay, so you decide who to move or cancel.

Typical uses:

- **Resurfacing a court** - close one padel court for the days the work takes, labelled "Resurfacing" on the schedule, and it takes bookings again once the period ends.
- **A public holiday or a camp** - close every area in the gym with a single entry, created weeks ahead, which also covers any court you add in the meantime.
- **A burst pipe** - block the rest of the day on the affected room, then see on the schedule who is already booked in that period and move or cancel them.

## Block or maintenance status?

Both take an area out of the booking flow, but they answer different questions.

| Use a block                                                 | Use **Maintenance** status                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| The area reopens on a known date                            | The area is out of action indefinitely               |
| You want the reason visible on the schedule for that period | You want the area off the schedule entirely          |
| Members should see the area again afterwards                | The area should stay hidden until you change it back |

Set **Maintenance** on the area itself (**Settings** > **Clubs** > **Areas**) when a space is out of action with no end in sight. Reach for a block whenever the period is known, which is most of the time.

<Note>
  Do not block a period by creating a placeholder booking. A booking is a
  financial record and will show up in your revenue reports. A block is not.
</Note>

## Creating a block

You create blocks from **Schedule**: click **Add** in the header, then **Block**.

In the **3 days** and **Week** views, the menu that opens when you click a slot also offers **Add Block** when the slot is tied to an area, with the area and the times pre-filled from what you selected. In the **Day** and **Timeline** views, clicking an area's lane opens the booking dialog instead, so use **Add** > **Block** there.

The dialog asks for:

- **Club** - which gym the block belongs to.
- **What is blocked** - either a single area, or **Whole club (all areas)** to close every area in that gym with one entry.
- **Start Time** and **End Time** - the period. These default to that day's opening and closing hours, so blocking a full day needs no editing. Change either one and your times are kept from then on.
- **Reason** - free text up to 500 characters, shown as the label on the schedule. "Resurfacing", "Camp", "Public holiday".

### Blocking a whole gym

Choose **Whole club (all areas)** rather than creating one block per court. One entry is easier to remove afterwards, and areas you add later are covered by it automatically - useful when a camp or holiday block is created weeks ahead.

## What a block looks like on the schedule

The blocked period appears as a grey, dashed card on the area's lane, labelled with your reason. It sits behind the day's real bookings rather than replacing them, so you can still see who is booked in the period you just blocked.

A gym-wide block shows on every one of that gym's area lanes in the day view. In the week and 3-day views, where all resources share one column per day, it shows as a single card so it can't crowd out the day's bookings.

Click the card to edit it, or use its menu to delete it. Deleting a block reopens the period immediately.

## Booking into a blocked period

Members cannot book a blocked period. The slots are simply not offered.

Staff who can manage bookings get a different result: the booking dialog says **Area blocked at this time** in its footer before they save, and **Add** books past it on the first click. That override exists for backfilling - recording a session that really happened before the block was entered - and the booking records who made it. Staff who can't manage bookings see that the area can't be booked, before they save.

If the blocked period is also at capacity - another booking or class already occupies it - going through requires a second step: a dialog names what's already there and requires a note before you can save. See [Warnings and scheduling conflicts](/schedule/bookings#warnings-and-scheduling-conflicts) for how that works and who can do it.

## Recurring blocks

Blocks do not repeat. A block is one concrete period.

For a pattern that repeats every week - closed every Monday morning, or a shorter Sunday - set the area's **Operating hours** instead (**Settings** > **Clubs** > **Areas**). Operating hours are the recurring schedule; blocks are the exceptions to it.

## Permissions

Reading blocks needs the same access as reading the schedule. Creating, editing, and deleting them needs permission to manage areas, which is the same permission that governs the areas themselves. Admins and managers have it by default; see [Roles and permissions](/reference/roles-permissions).

Overriding a block - or a genuine conflict - while creating a booking is governed separately, by permission to manage bookings. See [Warnings and scheduling conflicts](/schedule/bookings#warnings-and-scheduling-conflicts).
